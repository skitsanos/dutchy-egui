# DS-046 — Scribble pointer strokes record hand tremor as corners

Status: Resolved locally · 2026-11-07

## Problem

Scribble stored every primary-pointer sample at least 0.5 drawing units from
the previous one and drew the raw polyline. Tremor reached the stroke
unchanged: slow handwriting showed jagged edges, knots at cusps and a corner
per sample. A shaky 160-unit half circle (±1.5-unit tremor, 201 samples)
turned by up to 124° between consecutive segments. DS-025 and the coverage table listed smoothing as out of scope.

## Remediation

Add opt-in smoothing without changing the model or the three renderers:

- `ScribbleSmoothing::{Off, Light, Regular, Strong}` mirrors `ScribbleWidth`;
  stabilizer lengths 2/4/8 and a 0.25-unit tolerance are `tokens::scribble`
  values. `Scribble::smoothing` selects the level; the default stays `Off`, so
  existing hosts commit identical geometry.
- A distance-based pulled-string stabilizer filters pointer samples while
  drawing. Distance, not time, keeps results independent of event rate, frame
  timing and display scale; egui does not timestamp individual events.
- On commit, the stabilized points plus the last raw sample are fitted with a
  midpoint quadratic B-spline (no overshoot, endpoints kept), flattened within
  the tolerance, clamped to the samples' bounding box and simplified with
  Ramer–Douglas–Peucker in 256-point windows to bound commit cost.
- The live draft draws the same fitted curve.
- Keyboard strokes are never smoothed; a stabilizer would swallow 4-unit arrow
  steps and round deliberate corners.
- Points are baked in, so history, snapshots and SVG/raster/PNG exports stay
  ordinary polylines. If the smoothed stroke exceeds the remaining capacity,
  the capped stabilized samples are committed instead.

## Resolution

`src/scribble/smooth.rs` holds the pure pipeline shared by the widget and
`ScribbleSmoothing::smooth`. Unit tests cover the taut-string rule,
fitting tolerance and windowed simplification.

`tests/scribble_smoothing.rs` covers pass-through for `Off` and degenerate
input, and endpoint anchoring. On a shaky line, worst wobble falls from 1.49
to 1.18/0.93/0.69 units, with 111/65/43 of 251 points kept (Light/Regular/
Strong). On the shaky half circle the sharpest turn falls from 124° to
41°/20°/13°. A 2,000-step random walk clamped to the drawing edges stays in
bounds and pushes successfully at every level.

Widget tests show that committed pointer strokes equal `smooth()` of the
received samples at every level. Smoothing is captured at gesture start, the
next gesture uses the new level, clicks still make dots and keyboard strokes
keep exact grid positions with `Strong` selected.

A WGPU rendering check at 1x and 2x compares live and committed ink, allowing
for sub-pixel shifts: 0 pixels change on release. Raw and smoothed ink differ
by 398/713 and 1,618/2,859 pixels. With the preview's catch-up tail removed,
the check failed (8 pixels popped at 1x), so it detects a release jump.

Checks ran on Mesa lavapipe; existing Scribble baselines passed unchanged and
side-by-side 1x/2x renders were inspected. Check results are in
`docs/verification-scribble.md`.

Not addressed: tapered or variable-width ends, pressure, and egui's
`Shape::line` ends, which may differ from the round caps in exports. Ink trails
the pointer by up to the string length, and `Strong` closes loops smaller than
8 units. Native pointer and touch feel was not checked on hardware.
